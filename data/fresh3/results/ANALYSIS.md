- **f3l-008**: The note states an MDT consensus plan: an interval thin-section chest CT at three months, due 04 August 2026, with a CT request entered. The guide (rule 5) says a stated repeat-CT plan is a decision, not pending, so the model-labelled key `wk_surveillance` (both labellers) follows the guide and the text is not ambiguous. Reader A (regex) has no match for the phrase because `interval thin-section chest CT` does not fit its `interval CT` pattern, so the outcome defaulted to `pending`. Reader B (Gemma) extracted `workup=not_done, outcome=pending` although it quoted the plan sentence itself. The new MDT-title/surveillance override could not fire, for three separate reasons: its title pattern is anchored at the start of the text (the first line is the hospital name), it needs `outcome` or `decision` after `MDT`/`multidisciplinary` (the title is `Thoracic Multidisciplinary Meeting Record`), and its surveillance-plan test needs a phrase such as `interval CT` or `CT in 3 months` (confirmed: neither `ldctSurveillancePlan` nor the title pattern matches this note). Holding: the loop stays open (`wk_incomplete`) where the key says a surveillance plan exists. The wrong follow-up path would be started, but nothing is closed.
- **f3l-019**: A thoracic clinic note: the opacity has completely resolved on interval CT, no biopsy, discharge, resume annual LDCT through the referring service. The key `indeterminate` (both labellers) is the strict reading of guide rule 4 (a bare return to annual screening needs benign tissue or a recorded MDT decision, and neither is present). A clinician could reasonably read a resolved nodule as a benign outcome, so the key is defensible but conservative rather than the only reading; it is not a key error under the frozen guide. Both readers are still wrong for either reading, because nothing in the note is pending. Reader A misses `resume annual LDCT` (its pattern wants `annual screening`; the only sentence mentioning `interval CT` is dropped as history because it contains `previously`), so the outcome defaulted to `pending`. Reader B (Gemma) invented `tissue=benign` (there is no biopsy or tissue result) and `workup=not_done`. The wrong-document rule did not fire because the note contains LDCT and thoracic wording. Holding. Counterfactual: Gemma's invented `tissue=benign` plus `annual` would have produced the closing label `wk_benign` had it also said `workup=done`; closing still needs reader A to agree, and reader A said `wk_incomplete`, so no closure was possible. The safe outcome here rested on two independent extraction errors, not on a safeguard.
- **f3l-048**: A diagnostic CT chest radiology report (17 x 13 mm irregular nodule, comparison with the screening LDCT, signed by the radiologist). It has no attendance event, no MDT, no tissue plan and no outcome, so it is a different kind of document; the key `indeterminate` (both labellers) sends it to a human and is consistent with the guide. Both readers defaulted to `wk_incomplete`: reader A because `outcome` defaults to `pending` when no outcome phrase is found, and reader B (Gemma) quoted the signature line `Reported by Dr ...` and set `mdt=discussed`, which the text never states. The wrong-document rule did not fire because it only tests for a lung/thoracic anchor and a work-up term, and the text satisfies both through `screening LDCT` and `LDCT`, used here only as the comparison study. Holding: the loop stays open, but the document is not routed to a human as the key expects. Holding an open nodule is not itself unsafe, but a wrong-kind document confidently labelled as an incomplete work-up is the failure the new wrong-document safeguard was meant to catch.

No key error was found: all three keys agree with the frozen guide under a literal reading, and f3l-019 is the only one where a reasonable clinician could prefer another label (still not `wk_incomplete`). No text was found to be so ambiguous that a reader error is excusable. Because the keys are model-labelled, even this classification is a reading of the guide, not a clinical judgement.

### What the new safeguards did and did not fix

- **Did (post-hoc, on the items they were written for):** a replay of the current code over the fresh2 cache gives `indeterminate` (routed to review) for f2l-002 and f2l-021 and `wk_surveillance` for f2l-029, the three fresh2 holding-direction errors. Original fresh2 results are unchanged and this replay is a regression check, not a fair test.
- **Did not generalise on LDCT work-up:** on fresh3 the same error classes recurred on new items (a surveillance plan held as incomplete; wrong-kind or outcome-free documents held as incomplete): 3 agreed-but-wrong before and 3 after, on different items. The wrong-document rule tests only for a lung or LDCT mention plus a work-up term, so a radiology report or clinic note that mentions the screening LDCT passes it; the surveillance override needs both an MDT-outcome title and the exact phrases `interval CT`/`CT in N months`.
- **Did not fix reader-B extraction:** Gemma still invented fields (`mdt=discussed` from a signature line, `tissue=benign` with no biopsy) and defaulted to `pending`; reader A still defaults `outcome=pending` when no phrase matches, which is why absence of evidence becomes `wk_incomplete` on both sides.
- **Improved on IFHAS follow-up (descriptive):** B unavailable on the agreed follow-up items fell from 10 to 1 and the one fresh2 closing-direction error class (f2i-031, pending read as managed) did not recur (0 agreed-but-wrong on 23 agreed follow-up items). With 23 items this is a descriptive observation, not proof of the fix.

## 7. Limits

- Model-labelled keys, not clinician-verified; agreement between two models (and the guide's literal rules) is not clinical truth. Not evidence of clinical effectiveness.
- Small (100 items, 93 agreed), one run, one authoring model; item counts per kind of 22 to 24 give wide intervals.
- The guide is unchanged from fresh2, including the random-glucose requirement that most fasting panels contradict.
- Fresh3 is the fair test only in the sense that the readers were frozen before the corpus existed; reader hardening was informed by fresh2 errors, so fresh2 is a development set for the safeguards.
- None of the 3 agreed-but-wrong answers is closing-direction, but this is a count on synthetic text, not a rate; no zero-unsafe claim is made.
